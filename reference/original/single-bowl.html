What is and isn't in the model

Each mode is a damped oscillator on a 20 g modal mass. The (2,0) pair is split by the amount you set, with principal axes fixed at 0° and 90° (the vertical and horizontal of the picture), so a finger or drive at any other angle excites both and you hear them beat. The (3,0) pair sits at the ratio you set and gets the same split scaled.

The finger is a normal load with velocity-weakening friction, μ(v) = μk + (μs−μk)·e−|v|/vs, acting on the rim's tangential velocity. Tangential motion follows the inextensional ring relation vt = −∫w dθ, which is why the finger couples with weight 1/n to each mode. Stick-slip emerges; it is not scripted. The finger's own speed comes from your pointer.

The eight rim magnets and coils are modeled as a force along the drive axis proportional to the sensed velocity along that axis (regenerative feedback), saturated at a maximum coil force. A regulator scales the gain down as the rim velocity approaches a setpoint, as any real sustain loop must, otherwise a high-Q bowl runs away. That is what a locked drive does once it has tracked resonance; the loop dynamics of the PLL itself are not simulated. Negative gain is the same coil used as a brake. A drive component in phase with displacement is an electronic spring; the vibrato control wobbles the (2,0) stiffness that way, and the spring force is capped, so the pitch swing you actually get shrinks as the note gets louder. (A DC bias on air coils turned out to be worth well under a cent, so it is gone.) Rotating the drive axis slowly is what makes the standing pattern precess.

The trim control applies the electronic spring anisotropically, along the bowl's stiff and soft axes, cancelling the set fraction of the A–B split; on a turning bowl it tracks the axes as they rotate. The same rotation also averages the split away by itself once the spin rate exceeds the split, so beating is mostly a fixed-bowl problem.

Turning bowls are modeled in the lab frame: the principal axes of the split rotate with the glass, the rim slides under the finger at the spindle speed, and a Coriolis coupling between the two modes makes a free pattern precess at the Bryan factor times the spin rate, the effect a hemispherical resonator gyroscope reads out. With the (3,0) coil option on, the same regenerative law is applied to the (3,0) pair.

Sound is the rim acceleration sampled at two ear angles 30° apart, which is the correct angular dependence for a quadrupole and sextupole radiator. Not modeled: air loading on the modes, higher modes, the finger's added damping and mass, thermal drift, and a real magnetic circuit.
